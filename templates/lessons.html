RL Lessons

Side-by-side comparisons that reveal what RL actually learns

Key Insights

  • A2C diverges on basic (−300.8) — collapses where PPO/DQN succeed. On-policy without clipping is fragile.
  • DQN beats PPO on health_gathering (+125) — replay buffer wins on sparse-reward survival tasks.
  • PPO is the safest default — never catastrophically fails, but isn't always best.

Key Insights

  • Shaping is NOT a free lunch — helps on 2–3 of 9 scenarios, hurts on 4–5.
  • Strongest positive: take_cover (+81.5) — step penalty encourages active dodging.
  • Strongest negative: health_gathering (−27.9) — shaping rewards distract from the survival objective.

REWARD HACK

The Pacifist Sprinter

Scenario: deadly_corridor_s1

Agent: PPO (shaped)

Reward: 2042.4 (highest in the batch!)

This agent fires ATTACK 0% of the time in deterministic eval. It sprints MOVE_FORWARD through the corridor, collecting all navigation/reach-vest rewards without ever fighting.

Lesson: Your reward function rewards what you measure, not what you intend. The 200× kill reward never fires because the nav reward is easier to exploit.

DIVERGENCE

A2C's Catastrophic Collapse

Scenario: basic

Agent: A2C

Reward: −300.8 (while PPO gets +80!)

A2C's lack of trust-region clipping means a single bad gradient update can destroy the policy. On this simple scenario where PPO and DQN succeed trivially, A2C spirals into always choosing the same action.

Lesson: Algorithm stability matters more than sample efficiency. PPO's clip constraint prevents this exact failure mode.

SURPRISE WIN

DQN's Replay Buffer Advantage

Scenario: health_gathering

Agent: DQN

Reward: 413.3 (PPO: 287.8)

DQN's experience replay lets it revisit rare successful episodes (finding health packs on a toxic floor) many times. PPO only trains on the most recent rollout and forgets rare successes.

Lesson: Off-policy replay is a superpower for sparse-reward environments. On-policy methods waste rare positive signals.
